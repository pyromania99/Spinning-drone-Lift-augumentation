Spinning Quadrotor: Hover Thrust Augmentation
with Passive Lifting Surfaces

Aniketh Parkala, Harikumar Kandath
Robotics Research Center (RRC), IIIT Hyderabad
ICUAS 2026
Paper PDF DOI Code Videos Citation

Intentional steady rotation turns a quadrotor hover condition into an opportunity for passive lift augmentation — achieving a 22% reduction in required hover thrust.

Abstract

Conventional multirotor aerial vehicles actively suppress yaw rotation during hover, expending power to maintain a fixed heading despite the fact that yaw regulation is not required for force balance or altitude control. This paper challenges that paradigm by proposing a spinning quadrotor architecture that intentionally operates at a sustained yaw rate, converting power traditionally spent on yaw regulation into useful aerodynamic effects. A dynamic model of the spinning quadrotor is developed, and analysis for the low Reynolds-number range is conducted to choose an airfoil for the passive lifting surfaces. Preliminary hardware tests show a 22% reduction in thrust required. These findings suggest that intentional yaw rotation, rather than being suppressed, can be exploited as a design mechanism for efficient and robust multirotor flight.

How It Works

Four key stages take the concept from physical insight to verified hardware results.

01

Concept & Motivation

Traditional quadrotors burn energy fighting yaw torque imbalance to hold a fixed heading. We flip this: allow the body to spin freely and harness that rotation to generate passive lift via fixed-wing surfaces attached to the arms.

Yaw Energy Recovery Passive Lift Fixed-Wing Surfaces
02

Dynamic Modelling

A full rigid-body dynamic model of the spinning quadrotor is derived, accounting for gyroscopic effects, arm-mounted wing aerodynamics, and rotor thrust coupling under steady yaw-rate conditions.

Rigid-Body Dynamics Gyroscopic Effects Thrust Coupling
03

Airfoil Selection & BEMT / CFD Analysis

Low-Reynolds-number aerodynamic analysis (BEMT + CFD) is performed to select an airfoil profile optimised for the operating regime of the rotating arms, balancing lift coefficient, drag, and structural simplicity.

Low-Re Airfoils BEMT CFD Simulation
04

Hardware Validation

Tabletop and flight-test experiments confirm the model predictions. With passive lifting surfaces mounted, the platform achieves hover at 22% lower thrust, translating directly into extended endurance and payload capacity.
